0. THE STRATEGY (read this before touching PowerPoint)
==============================================================================

The requirements doc is explicit: judges should be hooked in the first 20-30
seconds, and should walk away thinking "this team understood the problem at a
level most teams didn't reach" — not "this team built the assigned features."

That difference is entirely about EVIDENCE and SPECIFICITY, not about having
more features. Three concrete rules, applied on every slide below:

RULE 1 — Never state a problem in the abstract. State it with a number and a
source. "GPS fails in tunnels" is a school-project sentence. "465 confirmed
GPS spoofing events in Indian airspace between Nov 2023-Feb 2025 (DGCA)" is a
judge-hook sentence. Every claim slide below has the number attached.

RULE 2 — Use the required formula, explicitly, at least twice:
    EXISTING SOLUTION → WORKS FOR → BUT FAILS WHEN → OUR SOLUTION → VALIDATED BY (source)
This is not a suggestion, it's what the requirements doc literally asks for.
Section 2 (below) below is written pre-formatted in exactly this shape —
lift it directly onto slides 2 and 4.

RULE 3 — Never inflate a number that hasn't been measured on THIS project.
CLAUDE.md's honesty rule extends to the pitch: when citing this project's own
results, cite only what STATUS_AND_ROADMAP.md/summary.txt actually recorded
(measured, on-device or on IO-VNBD-simulated outages). When citing published
research (IO-VNBD's own numbers, AVNet's <0.5% figure), attribute it clearly
to THAT paper, not to this project — the contrast between "SOTA achieves X"
and "we honestly haven't measured that yet, here's what we have measured" is
ITSELF a credibility signal to a judge who's seen ten teams overclaim tonight.

--- PAPER 1 (this project's own training data — use this first, it's airtight) ---
Onyekpe, U., Palade, V., Kanarachos, S., Szkolnik, A. "IO-VNBD: Inertial and
Odometry Benchmark Dataset for Ground Vehicle Positioning." Data in Brief,
2021 (also arXiv:2005.01701, 2020).
KEY FACT: ~58 hours / 4,400 km of real smartphone IMU+GPS driving data across
UK, Nigeria, and France, built specifically because "the lack of a public,
robust benchmark dataset hindered the advancement of ML techniques to learn
INS error." This is not a random citation — it is literally the dataset
ml/train_velocity_model.py and ml/train_reacquisition_model.py train against.
USE THIS TO SAY: "We didn't invent our own toy dataset to make our numbers
look good — we trained and evaluated against the same public benchmark the
research community uses, specifically so our results are comparable, not
cherry-picked."

--- PAPER 2 (state-of-the-art contrast — use this to justify YOUR architecture choice) ---
"Avnet: learning attitude and velocity for vehicular dead reckoning using
smartphone by adapting an invariant EKF." Satellite Navigation (Springer
Nature), 2025. DOI: 10.1186/s43020-025-00168-7.
KEY FACT: current academic SOTA for smartphone-only vehicular dead reckoning
uses a CNN+GRU deep neural network feeding an invariant Kalman filter, and
reports <0.5% horizontal positioning error of distance travelled in realistic
driving.
USE THIS TO SAY (this is your "we thought about the alternative and chose
deliberately" moment — huge credibility signal): "The current academic
state-of-the-art for this exact problem uses a deep neural network plus an
invariant Kalman filter to hit under 0.5% drift. We deliberately did NOT copy
that architecture — a CNN+GRU is expensive to run in real time on a mid-range
phone and its outputs aren't easily auditable. We chose lightweight,
explainable tree-ensemble models specifically because the official SIH bar is
under 10% drift, not under 0.5% — we're engineering to the actual requirement,
not chasing a bigger number for its own sake." This ALSO directly answers "why
didn't you use deep learning" before a judge asks it.

--- PAPER 3 (foundational grounding — smartphone VDR is a real, established research area) ---
Nawarathne, K., Zhao, F., Pereira, F.C., Luo, J. "Dead reckoning on
smartphones to reduce GPS usage." IEEE ICARCV 2014. DOI:
10.1109/ICARCV.2014.7064360.
USE THIS TO SAY: "Smartphone-only vehicular dead reckoning isn't a novel idea
we made up — it's been an active IEEE research area for over a decade. What's
still missing is a version of it shipped as a real, running, honestly-measured
consumer app, which is what we built."

--- GOVERNMENT DATA 1 (the urgency stat — THIS IS YOUR HOOK LINE, see Slide 2) ---
Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), Government of India — Advisory
Circular ANSS AC 01 of 2023 (24 Nov 2023) on GNSS Interference in Airspace;
subsequent reporting.
KEY FACTS:
  - 465 confirmed GPS spoofing events in Indian airspace, Nov 2023-Feb 2025,
    concentrated near the Pakistan border (Amritsar, Jammu).
  - 1,951 total GPS interference cases reported Nov 2023-Nov 2025.
  - DGCA has since mandated real-time reporting (within 10 minutes) of GNSS
    interference events, including a dedicated SOP for Delhi (IGI) airport
    (Nov 2025).
USE THIS TO SAY: "This isn't hypothetical. India's own aviation regulator
confirmed 465 GPS spoofing events in fifteen months, concentrated on the
border. GNSS denial is a documented, actively-monitored, worsening problem in
Indian airspace right now — and ground vehicles have none of the backup
inertial systems aircraft carry."
NOTE: this is aviation-sector data, not road-sector — be precise about that
when citing it (say "in Indian airspace," don't imply it's road data). Its
value is proving GNSS denial is a real, measured, current Indian phenomenon,
not an imported problem statement — not proving it happens on roads
specifically (the tunnel/underpass argument below covers roads).

--- GOVERNMENT DATA 2 (the "the problem is growing" stat) ---
NHAI / PIB (Press Information Bureau, Government of India) reporting, FY

KEY FACTS:

--- PRODUCT-FAILURE EVIDENCE (the "existing solution" half of the formula) ---
Google Maps / Waze official support-community threads and public reporting on
their tunnel-navigation feature (Waze/Google "Bluetooth beacon" tunnel
positioning, rolled out via TechCrunch/PhoneArena coverage and Google's own

KEY FACTS:
  - Waze/Google Maps's own documented fix for GPS loss in tunnels is NOT

  - Cost: reported at roughly $1,200 per mile of tunnel coverage (per-tunnel

  - Users on Google's own official support forum still report "GPS signal

USE THIS TO SAY (this IS the formula, verbatim — put this exact shape on a

cities that have paid to install Bluetooth beacons — but fails the moment
you're in a tunnel without that installed infrastructure, which is every
tunnel in India, including the 68 NHAI is building in Himachal Pradesh right
now. Our solution needs zero installed infrastructure — it works from the
sensors already inside the phone, in any tunnel, anywhere — validated by

stays to a handful of cities worldwide."
This is your single strongest slide moment. It reframes the entire pitch from

SLIDE 3 — TECHNICAL APPROACH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Template fields: Technologies to be used, Methodology/process (flow
charts/images/working prototype).

INSTRUCTION FROM REQUIREMENTS DOC: don't just list tool names — say WHY each
one is used, specifically for this project. Table format, not a bullet list
of logos:

    Kotlin + Jetpack Compose
        WHY: native Android sensor access at guaranteed low latency: ML/DR
        math cannot run on a scripting/cross-platform layer at 10Hz without
        jank — this has to be native to hit the sample rate.

    SensorManager (Accelerometer, Gyroscope, Rotation Vector, Barometer)
        WHY: these are the ONLY sensors in scope — deliberately no external
        IMU, no OBD-II. The barometer specifically is used for floor/level
        change detection in multi-level parking, a case regular DR alone
        can't distinguish.

    FusedLocationProviderClient (GNSS)
        WHY: Android's production-grade location API, not raw GNSS chip
        polling — gives accuracy/HDOP metadata this project's honesty checks
        depend on (see Slide 4).

    ONNX Runtime (on-device inference)
        WHY: runs the trained velocity + drift-prediction models directly on
        the phone, zero network dependency, zero cloud round-trip latency —
        the DR loop has to keep functioning specifically WHEN connectivity is
        unreliable, so a server-dependent model would defeat the point.

    scikit-learn (Gradient Boosted Trees / Random Forest / LinearRegression) —
    trained offline in Python, exported to ONNX
        WHY: chosen over a deep neural network (see AVNet comparison, Slide
        2) specifically for on-device feasibility and explainability — every
        prediction is traceable to a named feature, not a black box.

    osmdroid + OSM Mapnik / Nominatim / OSRM
        WHY: open, no-API-key mapping/geocoding/routing stack — keeps the
        prototype's cost at zero and avoids a dependency on a paid SDK key
        during development (Google Maps SDK is a planned swap once available).

METHODOLOGY / FLOW CHART: Reuse the architecture pipeline from PRD.md Section
10 as an actual flow diagram graphic (not text) — Sensors → Preprocessing →
Alignment/Classification/Velocity(ML) → State Estimator (ZUPT+NHC) →
GNSS-fusion / Dead-Reckoning branch → Map-constraint → UI. This is the same
diagram in pitch.txt Section 3 — render it as boxes+arrows, not a bullet list.

HOW ML AND PHYSICS WORK TOGETHER (put this as a labeled two-track diagram,
this is likely to get a direct judge question so make it visually clear):
    PHYSICS TRACK (always running, the fallback):
        heading[t] = heading[t-1] + gyro_z*dt (bias-corrected, aligned)
        v[t] = ML velocity, or physics integration if ML is out-of-distribution
        position[t] = position[t-1] + v[t]*(cos,sin)(heading[t])*dt
        Gated by ZUPT (kills drift while stationary) and the non-holonomic
        constraint (a car can't move sideways — suppresses lateral noise).
    ML TRACK (used where it demonstrably helps, per PRD Rule 3):
        Velocity Estimator — regression, replaces raw accelerometer double-
        integration with a learned forward-velocity estimate.
        Motion Classifier — 8 classes (Stationary/Moving/Accelerating/
        Braking/Pothole/Turning/Cruising/Phone Moved), each with a real
        downstream effect (e.g. Pothole discounts a shock spike from being
        misread as forward acceleration).
        Reacquisition Drift Model — predicts expected drift magnitude to set
        an ADAPTIVE GNSS-reacquisition blend duration instead of a fixed one.
    SAY: "Physics is the floor — it always runs, so the system never fully
    breaks even if ML is wrong. ML is layered on top only where it measurably
    beats the physics-only baseline — we have the comparison numbers to prove
    it, not just the claim."
